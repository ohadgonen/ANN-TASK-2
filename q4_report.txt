Question 4 - augmentation

Adding augmentation helped to solve the overfitting problem, because for every epoch
the model is trained on slightly different data. This means that specific features aren't
memorized, enabling greater reproducibility to the testing data.

Comparing the performance to previous models:
Q1 (basic CNN) vs Q4 (CNN + augmentation + full BatchNorm):

The network architecture is identical in both: four convolutional layers (32 -> 64 -> 128 -> 256
channels) each followed by max-pooling, then two fully-connected layers (256 -> 10). The
differences are purely in training conditions and normalization coverage.

Q1 only had BatchNorm after the first conv layer. The remaining three conv layers (conv2,
conv3, conv4) had no normalization, so as gradients propagated back through them the
activation distributions could shift freely between batches. This made training less stable
in the deeper layers and offered no regularization benefit beyond the first layer.

Q4 adds BatchNorm after every conv layer. This keeps activations in a consistent range
throughout the full depth of the network, which stabilizes gradients and acts as a
regularizer at each stage of the feature hierarchy. The result is faster and more consistent
convergence across epochs.

Q1 was trained on unmodified images (only ToTensor + Normalize). The model would see the
exact same pixel values for each image every epoch and could overfit to specific spatial
arrangements in the training set. Q4 adds RandomCrop, RandomRotation, RandomResizedCrop,
and RandomHorizontalFlip to the training pipeline. Each epoch the model sees a different
transformed version of each image, which prevents memorization of exact pixel layouts and
forces the model to learn features that generalize across positions, scales, and orientations.

Together, these changes reduce the train/test accuracy gap visible in the loss curves: Q1
shows the two curves diverging as training progresses, while Q4 keeps them closer together
throughout, which is the direct signature of reduced overfitting.


Changes over the starter code:

1. Data augmentation on training set: Added RandomCrop(32, padding=4), RandomRotation(15),
   RandomResizedCrop(32, scale=(0.8, 1.0)), and RandomHorizontalFlip() to train_transform.
   These transformations synthetically increase dataset diversity so the model sees different
   crops, orientations, and scales of each image during training, reducing overfitting. The
   test transform is unchanged (only ToTensor + Normalize).

2. BatchNorm on all conv layers: Added nn.BatchNorm2d after every convolutional layer (conv1
   through conv4). Previously only conv1 had BatchNorm. Normalizing activations at each layer
   stabilizes training, reduces internal covariate shift, and acts as a regularizer throughout
   the full feature hierarchy rather than just at the first layer.

3. Replaced Tanh/Sigmoid with ReLU: Changed the activation function in all layers to F.relu.
   ReLU avoids the vanishing gradient problem that sigmoid/tanh suffer from in deeper networks,
   and typically leads to faster convergence.
